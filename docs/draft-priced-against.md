# Draft, not published: what a reader should be told about the $29 scanners

**Status: a draft. Nothing here is on the site and none of it goes up without Krystian saying so.**
`/pricing` currently says, in its own words, that we have not independently measured anybody else's
product. That sentence is what makes this draft hard to write and worth writing carefully: naming a
competitor is publishing a claim about a third party, and the only safe material is what they publish
about themselves.

## What is verified, and how

Read from `https://agentable.is` on 2026-08-19 with a browser user agent, quoted from their own page
rather than from anybody's summary of it:

- **MONITOR, $29/mo, per domain**: weekly automated re-scans, email alerts when the score regresses
  or a MUST check breaks, score history, an embeddable badge.
- **AGENCY, $99/mo**, monitoring for up to 25 client domains plus CSV export.
- Their footer describes the product as a static spec-compliance scan that, in their words,
  "verifies artifacts exist, not live agent success".

Our own catalogue, for the same comparison: monitoring $79/mo per domain, agency $499/mo for 10.

## The paragraph, if we publish one

> Priced against a $29 scanner? The weekly half of what we do is comparable to one, and we would not
> argue you should pay more for it. The other half is not on offer at that price: five times a month
> we put a buying question to an agent in an empty directory, count who it named instead of you, and
> quote the sentence it named them with. A scanner that checks whether a file exists cannot tell you
> that a run picked somebody else and why. Neither can we tell you it will pick you if you fix a row,
> and we publish which two of our sixteen checks we can show any relationship for.

## What must be true before it goes up

1. Somebody has to decide we are willing to name a competitor at all. Today we are not.
2. Every number in it has to come from their published page on the day we publish, not from this
   file. Prices move.
3. The agency line is the one that does not survive contact: $499 for 10 domains is $49.90 each
   against their $3.96, so a paragraph about value per domain invites exactly the arithmetic we lose.
   Either the agency tier moves or the paragraph does not mention packs.
